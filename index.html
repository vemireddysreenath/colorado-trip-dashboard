Corrections to the brief

What changed and why

Monday geography

Tabernash is near RMNP's west entrance (Grand Lake). Bear Lake is on the east side. The only direct way there is Trail Ridge Road, about 2 hours each way. Trail Ridge is the route itself, not an optional extra. If it closes, Bear Lake turns into a 5½-hour round trip around the park. A west-side backup plan is below.

Bear Lake permit

The brief listed it as "check requirements." For Oct 12 a Timed Entry + Bear Lake Road permit is required from 5 AM to 6 PM. You enter the park before 9 AM, so you don't need the general timed-entry permit (Oct 12 is its last day), but the Bear Lake Road permit is a separate requirement.

Friday arrival

About 605 miles and 9½ hours of driving, plus stops. Leaving at 1 PM Central puts you in Trinidad around 10:30–11 PM Mountain, not 8–9. The stretch after Amarillo is in the dark, and deer and pronghorn are common there.

Sunday loop

Drive to Aspen on the interstate via Glenwood Springs, then come back over Independence Pass. The ghost town sits on that road, so you don't backtrack. Take the shuttle rather than your own car, because the parking rules would force a 4 AM start.

Option B honesty

The extra Durango night doesn't remove the 13-hour drive home. Durango to Dallas is about as far as Tabernash to Dallas. Option B adds a great scenic day and moves the long drive to Wednesday. It's still the better choice. Just plan Wednesday as a full driving day.

Fall color

2026 color was early and patchy, with most of the state peaking around Oct 1. By Oct 10–12, aspens at Maroon Bells, Independence Pass, and Trail Ridge (9,500 ft and up) are likely past peak or bare. The mountains are still the reason to go. The best remaining gold should be lower down: Glenwood Canyon, the Roaring Fork valley, and Ridgway, Ouray, and Durango on Tuesday.

Holiday crowds

Mon Oct 12 is Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples' Day, so Saturday through Monday is a 3-day weekend. Parking lots will fill early. The schedule below has you arriving before the rush.

If Pikes Peak is closed or icy

The road is often open only partway (to Glen Cove or Devils Playground) after snow. Take what's open, or ride the Pikes Peak Cog Railway from Manitou if tickets are available. Or spend more time in Garden of the Gods and Manitou, then leave early for Tabernash.

Why this order

The Garden opens at 5 AM and Pikes doesn't open until 9. This order uses the morning well and keeps the Pikes visit far from the 3 PM cutoff.

No Bear Lake permit

  • The park's free shuttle runs from the Park & Ride on Bear Lake Road through Oct 18. Get there before about 9 AM because the lot fills on the holiday. Ask the ranger at the Bear Lake Road station which permit the Park & Ride needs that day.
  • Or skip it and see Lily Lake (flat 0.8-mile loop on CO 7, no permit), Sheep Lakes, and the Alluvial Fan.

Trail Ridge closed

Don't make the 5½-hour drive around the park. Do a west-side day instead: Kawuneeche Valley wildlife, the flat 1-mile Coyote Valley Trail, Adams Falls (0.6 mile round trip), and Grand Lake town. Plan it for an easy afternoon.

Traveling with a young child

Kid, altitude, and car notes

Altitude

  • Tabernash sits at about 8,300 ft. Expect the first night to be restless.
  • Water all day, every day. Fill bottles at every stop.
  • Above 12,000 ft (Pikes, Trail Ridge, Independence), keep stops short. A headache, vomiting, or unusual sleepiness in the child means drive down. Descending fixes it.

Cold and sun

  • Expect 70s in the valleys and 20s–40s with wind on the summits on the same day.
  • Pack hats, gloves, and a puffy jacket for everyone.
  • Bring sunscreen and sunglasses. UV is strong at altitude.

In the car

  • Carsickness on switchbacks: sit the child where they can see out the front, keep snacks bland, and bring a bag just in case.
  • Download offline maps for CO 82, Trail Ridge, US 550, and US 87. There's no signal for long stretches.
  • Refill the gas tank at half on mountain days.

Wildlife

  • Elk rut is underway in RMNP and Grand County. Bulls are aggressive. Stay 75 ft or more away and watch from the car.
  • Dawn and dusk drives (Fri night, Sun morning) are prime time for animals on the road.
